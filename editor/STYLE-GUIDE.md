# Project copy standard

Every project description is a **STE001 statement declaration**.

`STE001 — [Artifact] [does a specific thing] [with a concrete input, output, constraint, result, or source].`

Example: `STE001 — The extension records browser-visible search queries and exports prompt/query pairs as CSV.`

## Required rules

1. Do not use first-person voice (`I`, `we`, `my`, `our`) in AI-related prose or quoted model material.
2. Do not present a personal author persona. Replace “Eric built…”, “a project by Eric”, or an identity claim with the project fact itself.
3. Describe the artifact. Lead with what it does, then explain implementation, evidence, and limits. Do not lead with a feeling, a market pitch, or a claim of importance.
4. Treat model output as source material, not portfolio narration. Quote it only when the transcript itself is the project evidence; otherwise summarize the behavior and link the artifact.
5. Give every current numerical, price, provider, or capability claim a date, source, measurement, or explicit scope.

## House voice

Write with the plain, observed tone of a 2016 technical product report: direct lead, specific nouns and verbs, concrete inputs and outputs, implementation details where useful, and a stated limitation when there is one. Cut promotional adjectives, self-commentary, and generic framing.

Use: “The parser reads ITCH messages from a byte buffer and validates message lengths before decoding.”

Avoid: “This robust, cutting-edge project unlocks a seamless new way to parse market data.”
