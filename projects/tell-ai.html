How to tell if written work is AI

Easily tell if something is AI using these obvious giveaways

AI-written text keeps showing up in more places. This is not to shame anyone who uses AI. These are just a few tells that you can use to know if something is AI, so you can say, "Hey, this is probably AI!" and then do nothing else. AI-generated READMEs and summarizations are convenient, and Eric Spencer uses them too. These are observations that have turned up more and more within the past year.

It's not just X, It's Y!

This sentence structure turns up more and more often in content online. It was not noticeable before a couple of months ago. So, maybe more and more people are generating summaries with AI that use this sentence structure. It already gets old.

Others have noticed this too

📝 Emojis

Asking ChatGPT to generate a markdown file with a list of topics is an easy way to prove this one. It will often give you a title with an emoji next to it. Nobody would go out of their way to mark EVERY topic with a custom emoji for something such as a summary of what you think about B2B SaaS tools, the kind that shows up constantly on LinkedIn. The emojis on most lists of topics are pleasant enough. An emoji provides a visual cue of what the topic is in your peripheral vision, something that does a better job than a word or two about the topic. The only problem is having the correct emoji for your topic.

The em dash

The em dash drew suspicion long before AI. Using one in a freshman-year high school English paper (~7 years ago) already carried the worry that the teacher would think a source had been copied from the internet. It was not something taught in class, just something picked up from reading articles online. So, for it to be a telltale sign of AI use today is no surprise at all.

That is a reason to be cautious with it now, and to use a stupid amount of parenthesis instead for a sidepoint during a sentence (like right here).
