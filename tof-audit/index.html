Time-of-Flight Audit

Light in fiber travels about 200 km per millisecond. That sets a hard floor on how fast this browser can reach any given city — and no VPN, proxy, or edited location setting can move that floor. Run the probe and see what the wire says.

Notes

How it reads. Each probe opens a connection to a server in a known city and times the round trip. Divide by two, multiply by 200 km/ms, and you get the furthest that server could possibly be. Do it against eight cities at once and the overlap of those ranges is a circle you are standing inside. Congestion and server delay only ever make a link look longer — so a short round trip is proof of nearness that cannot be faked in software.

What it can't do. Estimates land within a few hundred kilometres at best, and routing that detours (satellite links, corporate tunnels, congested peering) inflates every reading and pushes the guess outward. Treat a mismatch as a question, never an answer.

Runs entirely in your browser — nothing is sent anywhere or stored. Eric Spencer
